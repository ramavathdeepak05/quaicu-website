## 1. The Core Framework

| Level | What the content contains | What the audience does | QUAICU translation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Post** consistently | Ignores you | Another agency posting "we build apps and AI" |
| 2 | + Their **Problems** | **Notices** you | "They know our last vendor was great for two months, then slipped" |
| 3 | + Useful **Solutions** | **Pays attention** | "They showed me the early signs a vendor is decaying, and what to put in the contract" |
| 4 | + A new **Perspective** | Sees you as an **Authority** | "Judge a vendor on month twelve, not month one" |
| 5 | + **Don't Ask** for anything | **Waits** for your offer | "When we need a team that stays good, we'll call QUAICU" |

**Standard:** every post hits Level 3. One flagship piece a week (the blog post) hits Level 5.

**Why this angle (from the research):** complaints about agencies were the largest cluster found (about 42 tagged mentions): missed deadlines, ghosting, status calls that hide slipped milestones, project managers and developers swapped mid-project, hidden subcontracting, code held hostage. That is service decay, described by buyers. Governance (named reviewer, record nobody can edit) is the *mechanism* that stops it, and the internal AI agent is the *engine* that keeps it consistent. Neither is the headline.

## 7. The Post Formula

```
HOOK      → Name the problem in their words              (Level 2)
CONTEXT   → Why it happens / what it costs them
FIX       → 2–4 concrete, usable steps                   (Level 3)
TWIST     → The perspective others won't say             (Level 4)
CLOSE     → A takeaway or a question. Never a pitch.     (Level 5)
```

### Worked example (LinkedIn, founder voice, problems #1–3)

```
HOOK     Your agency was brilliant in month one. What happened by month six?
CONTEXT  The senior people who pitched you moved to the next new client. The
         weekly call is still there, but the demos stopped. Dates slip by a
         few days, then a few weeks.
FIX      Three checks for any vendor you already work with:
         1. Ask to see what shipped this month, not what's planned.
         2. Ask who reviewed it, by name.
         3. Check the repo, hosting and accounts are in your name.
TWIST    Decay isn't bad luck. It's what happens when quality depends on
         who's having a good week. It should depend on the process.
CLOSE    Which month did your last vendor start slipping?
```

### Hook templates
- "Your agency was great in month one. What happened by month six?"
- "6 signs your software vendor is starting to slip."
- "Nobody tells you this about outsourcing: the team that pitched you isn't the team that stays."
- "Stop judging vendors on the first sprint. Judge them on the fiftieth ticket."
- "Only 10–15% of AI pilots reach production in India. Here's what the rest are missing."
- "Before you sign with a dev agency, check these 4 things in the contract."
- "Everyone says AI makes delivery faster. We use it to make delivery *consistent*."

### Close templates (no selling)
- "Which month did your last vendor start slipping?"
- "Save this for your next vendor review."
- "Send this to whoever manages your tech vendors."
- "The short version: judge the vendor on month twelve."
