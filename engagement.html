10 ENGAGEMENT

How we engage.
Every step, in order.

Every QUAICU product engagement begins with the diagnostic. Everything else, the governance roadmap, the deployment, the production support, follows from what the diagnostic finds. This is the arc.

FOUR STAGES · ONE WRITTEN HANDSHAKE AT EACH GATE
10·1 THE ARC

Four stages, in sequence.

STAGE 01 · PRODUCTISED

The Diagnostic

On-site engagement across eight departments. Final report within 15 to 20 days. The only fixed-shape stage. No product pitch during the audit. Methodology is ours; findings belong to you.

Read the diagnostic brief
STAGE 02 · YOURS TO KEEP

Governance Roadmap

Post-diagnostic. A 90-day plan that prioritises what to fix first, what to stop immediately, and what to sequence next. The roadmap is yours whether you continue with QUAICU or not.

STAGE 03 · CUSTOM-SCOPED

Pilot or Deployment

Modules selected from the roadmap. Co-designed with your team. Deployable on-premise, in your private cloud, or hybrid. Sequencing chosen by risk and return, not vendor convenience.

STAGE 04 · ONGOING

Production

Once live, QUAICU is accountable for uptime, version updates, kernel patches, and the audit packs your regulators ask for. Your governance kernel runs continuously.

GATE 01 → 02

Final diagnostic report delivered. You decide whether to commission the governance roadmap.

GATE 02 → 03

Roadmap accepted. You decide which modules to pilot, with whom, and on what posture.

GATE 03 → 04

Pilot signed off. Production rollout begins under a written operating agreement.

EVERY GATE

A written handshake. You can stop here; the work done up to this point is yours.

10·3 WHAT VARIES

What we shape together, per institution.

Every pilot is custom-scoped. The diagnostic gives us the operational truth; the conversation that follows shapes everything below.

01

Duration

Depends on institutional scale, departments in scope, and module count. The diagnostic itself is a 15 to 20 day cycle; deployment timelines are scoped against the roadmap.

02

Modules

Chosen from the roadmap, sequenced by risk and return. Most institutions begin with two or three modules and expand as the audit ledger stabilises.

03

Buying committee

Who signs, who approves on the line-of-business side, who owns audit. We adjust the diagnostic agenda to surface what each role needs to see.

04

Cost shape

The diagnostic is a fixed engagement. Deployment is priced against operational burden removed, not tokens consumed. No per-seat surprises.

05

Approvals and gates

Each stage ends with a written handshake. You see what was delivered, what is recommended next, and what we will not proceed with until you sign off.

06

Regulatory frame

Different verticals operate under different regulators. NAAC, NBA, UGC, ABDM, RERA, RBI, DPDP. The diagnostic surfaces which apply to your institution today, and which are coming.

NEXT: WHEN WE RECOMMEND NOT PROCEEDING
10·4 OFF-RAMPS

When we recommend not proceeding.

The diagnostic exists so that neither side commits to software that is not the right intervention. Three honest scenarios:

  • → If the governance gap turns out to be cultural rather than technical, QUAICU is not the right next step. We will say so in the final report.
  • → If the institution wants speed over accountability, we are the wrong vendor. The kernel is built to slow down AI that should not act, not to accelerate AI that should not have been approved.
  • → If a vertical regulator is mid-transition, with guidance still pending, we may recommend deferring deployment for a quarter. Better to deploy under final rules than to retrofit later.
WHY OFF-RAMPS MATTER

Vendors who never recommend against a sale make decisions easier in the short run and harder in the long run. The institutions we deploy with stay deployed. The institutions we walk away from often come back later, when the timing is right.
